# Changelog

Every released version of **UNSC Reborn**, newest first. This is the same
text as the [forum thread](https://fractalsoftworks.com/forum/index.php?topic=35803.0); it lives here so it can be linked and
searched.

## v0.6 - "It Was Locked for a Reason"
*"You do not post four hundred warriors on the inside of a door you intend to open."*

- **There is an excavation, and it is the spine of the release.** Everything in this mod has been a ladder - devotion, clearance, rank, relics, the tithe - all of it ratcheting upward forever and resolving into a larger number, which is why you could finish a campaign without ever feeling it went anywhere. There is now one thing with a direction: a shaft under a Covenant world called Sanctum, sunk on a fixed bearing, two years deep and still going. Six strata, each announcing itself, ending at a constructed surface the Ministry is calling a floor
- **Depth is computed, not accumulated.** It is recalculated from first principles every tick out of days elapsed and relics surrendered, rather than being a running total that gets added to. No drift, no double-counting from a listener firing twice - and because the inputs are already the truth, the depth can never disagree with what the Vault says it has. It also means retuning the pacing later corrects saves that already exist
- **Section Three will put a team on the shaft head** and cost them a season. Omega clearance, eighty marines aboard, twenty-four of whom do not come back, and ninety days before anyone will sign for another one. It is the only thing on that board that moves a number nobody else can reach
- **The Ministry will let you escort the survey convoys** and take a season off in the other direction. It costs a hold of supplies, the Hierarchy does not reimburse the faithful for the privilege of being useful, and when the lighters open you see that the crews going down are not engineers - they are carrying the same instruments you have been handing to the Vault
- **Relic convoys fly between the anchorage and the shaft** and can be intercepted. The same lever expressed as a fleet in a system rather than a button in a menu: it can be hunted, missed, or watched going past while you decide you cannot take it
- **The Vault's findings are a dig record instead of a loyalty card.** They used to unlock on relics surrendered, which meant the Ministry told you things for handing over enough. They now publish as the shaft reaches each stratum. Surrendering still brings them sooner, because relics move the dig - it is just no longer the only thing that does
- **Hoarding relics has consequences.** Surrendering them bought rank at no price for three versions, so everybody did, because there was never a reason not to. The Ministry knows what your Array reported and can see the gap: standing bleeds, then your commission is withdrawn without a reason being given, then a recovery lance is detached and it is not coming to talk about the manifest
- **You can go down the shaft,** and who is waiting depends on which ladder you climbed. A Covenant player finds Rtas 'Kaidonee at the face with his helmet off, working out one sentence ahead of you what four hundred warriors are doing eleven kilometres underground. Anyone holding omega finds four people with ONI equipment and unmarked crates who have been down there eight months and will tell you that you will not find the file
- **The door opens outward.** That is the whole reveal and it takes a moment to arrive. The seals are the same alloy as the relics - not similar, the same - and whoever built it scattered the instruments that closed it across forty systems so no one party could ever hold enough at once. The Vault has however many of yours you gave it
- **Three endings, and all three now do something.** Bring the shaft down and the dig stops permanently, buying a decade nobody will thank you for. Open it and something that has waited a hundred thousand years comes up eleven kilometres of shaft. Or hand the reading to the people who are not currently digging, and somebody other than you starts interdicting the convoys
- **The Flood, and they are their own faction now rather than a palette.** Nine infested hulls in dull olive with the screens shunted: this mod has always said a Covenant ship IS its shield and that the hull behind it cannot take a hit, so a Flood one is brittle, relentless, and dangerous in numbers rather than in a duel. Three of the nine are UNSC - a Marathon, a Halcyon and an Autumn, still carrying their own spinal guns, because the Covenant infesting Covenant was only ever the less frightening half of the idea
- **Infestation Bloom,** a ship system of their own. It repairs hull, which nothing else in the mod does, and it stops at eighty per cent - the biomass knits the ship back to most of a ship and never to a whole one. That is both the flavour and the reason it cannot outheal focused fire
- **The Flood have their own faces.** They were using the Covenant's portraits unaltered, which was the one place the faction most obviously had nothing of its own. Six now, infested, and drawn from both sides - three Covenant hosts and three human ones
- **Quarantine sites in the outer rim.** Three remote systems holding an outbreak somebody already contained: derelict infested hulls you can recover, a battle debris field, and a picket that has not moved in a very long time and still fires. Fixed strength, no respawn - a place rather than a treadmill. They are the end of an outbreak, not the start of one, which is what makes the door under Sanctum matter
- **A twelfth mission: "The Door Has Hinges."** You take the Ministry's own battlegroup, standing on the shaft head when the frame relaxes. Not the UNSC - handing the player the UNSC makes it a rescue, and handing them the people who dug makes it the bill arriving. The infested human hulls come up first
- **The clock can run out.** Reach the bottom without resolving the shaft and the Covenant open it themselves. Previously the excavation could complete, print one sentence, and the game would carry on as though a hundred thousand year old quarantine had not just been opened by people who thought it was a tomb
- **Rtas 'Kaidonee is a contact,** and the Great Schism finally pays out. That choice has been asked since 0.5 with nothing on either side of it: report him and he is gone, go with him and every Covenant port shuts. Stand with him now and you get a Sangheili shipmaster who will quietly stop a convoy for you - abstracted rather than a visible battle, because one man acting against the fleet he belonged to only works while nobody is looking. He is reachable from anywhere, which the Covenant campaign has never had an equivalent of
- **The Covenant have moved five systems further out.** They sat close enough to Epsilon Eridani that a starting fleet drifting the obvious direction met a faction hostile to everything that fields nothing under a cruiser. They are not balanced as an early threat and were never meant to be
- **Four things worth finding on the Covenant side.** They had two special items against the UNSC's nine, which left that whole career with almost nothing to pick up. A Reclamation Culture that lifts the vats, a Ministry Sacrament Pattern that lifts the workshops, a Sangheili honour blade, and a stratum sample cut from the shaft and sealed where it was cut
- **The ONI dossier carries the excavation.** The dig announces each stratum as a notification and a notification is gone the moment it scrolls; the Covenant standing entry has had a persistent version since the dig was built, but that only exists for a player the Ministry has ranked - so the UNSC player, who is the one Section One warns, had nowhere to go and read it again
- **The Flood fire their own guns now.** Every weapon on an infested hull was borrowed - twelve Covenant, ten UNSC, zero native. Three are theirs: the Spore Battery, the Bile Mortar and the Lash, grown over the Covenant hardpoints they replaced, in the faction's own sickly light. The infested UNSC hulls keep their MACs, because a Flood Marathon firing a human spinal gun is the whole horror of it
- **A Spartan.** The mod has carried the portrait since 0.1 and never the person. Reach omega clearance and Section Three transfers an officer whose file is one long redaction, carrying SPARTAN-II Conditioning - the one skill no level-up will ever offer anyone else
- **The dig is tunable in-game.** With LunaLib installed, the excavation length, the relic weighting, the Flood emergence rate and the balance telemetry are sliders under LunaLib's settings. Without LunaLib nothing changes - the shipped pacing is the default, and because depth is computed rather than accumulated, moving a slider corrects a running campaign
- **Covenant escorts cost what they are worth.** Measured per deployment point, their light hulls were twice as durable as the UNSC's at every hit strength - capital-grade screens on picket ships at picket prices. Escort screens now run at escort efficiency and the deployment costs moved: the gap is a third of what it was, and it survives the no-one-shot rule
- **Four codex entries for the things you keep almost working out** - the excavation, the quarantine contacts, the bloom, and the Schism
- **Reach, Sanctum and the quarantine sites have faces.** Docking views for the graveyard world, the forge world with the shaft head burning on its nightside, and an infested hull adrift where somebody contained an outbreak
- **Version Checker support.** The mod now announces its own updates to anyone running Version Checker
- Every Flood hull and weapon has a proper description. The whole faction had none, which no checker looked for - there is a checker for it now
- The pacing is a stated decision rather than two magic numbers: three in-game years untouched, and thirty-one relics to halve that. Nobody has played long enough to validate it, and the telemetry reader refuses to derive anything from console-driven testing, which is exactly what it is for
- Audio is 28 MB smaller. The Covenant music was encoded at 363-439 kbps against the UNSC music's 116-128, and one eight-minute track was 24 MB on its own - a third of the mod. Everything now lands on the standard half the mod already met
- **Every campaign screen got a voice pass - all 283 of them.** The registers were already strict: the liaison contracts, the Covenant never do, the Kig-Yar quartermaster is the one exception because she is contracted, not converted. But the prose had tells - six stage directions each reused across three unrelated scenes, both job boards confirming every contract with the identical sentence, a rank recitation that read like it was being read. The reused lines are gone, the boards answer four different ways, and the stiffest screens sound like somebody talking instead of somebody quoting
- The voice checker thinks in scenes now. Rules answering the same dialogue option are one screen in different states - the three arrivals at the shaft bottom, the Ministry's rank tiers - so reuse between them is design, not a tic. It stopped flagging those and got stricter about everything else, and the whole corpus passes it clean
- One cached lookup for Sanctum. Three scripts each resolved it independently and none remembered the answer, walking every market in the sector five or six times a tick to answer a question whose answer never changes

**Fixes**
- **Fixed: the Flood was fielding clean Covenant ships.** Its roster listed sixty-two Covenant variants and not one Flood variant, and both spawners build through FleetFactoryV3, which reads roles rather than known hulls. The six infested hulls were listed as known and then never drawn for anything - so the thing that comes up the shaft at the end of the campaign was a Covenant battlegroup with the Flood's name on it. Only the derelicts at the quarantine sites were genuinely Flood, because those are placed by explicit id, which is exactly why it never looked broken
- **Fixed: two Flood hulls had dead ship systems.** Skins inherit their system from the base hull silently, so the Infested CAS was running a SHIELD_MOD on a ship whose shields had been shunted - it did nothing at all - and the Infested CRS kept a weapon-flux penalty while losing the shield absorption that paid for it
- **Fixed: the excavation would have been frozen at zero in every save.** It looked for a market called cov_sanctum. Market ids here are the planet id plus "market", so it found nothing, decided the Covenant did not hold the world, and stopped the clock before it started - without throwing, which is the part that would have hurt
- **Fixed: "bring the shaft down on it" did not bring the shaft down.** It removed fourteen depth, so the dig climbed back to the door inside a season while the prose claimed eleven kilometres of rock had landed on it
- **Fixed: opening the ONI dossier crashed the intel screen.** A per-cent sign written into a format string parsed as a specifier and threw at render time, taking the whole panel with it
- **Fixed: escorting the survey convoy was a money printer** before it shipped - repeatable, paid, and worth more in credits than the supplies it consumed
- **Fixed: two special items crashed the game on load.** Both were trade goods with no industry behaviour, so the plugin column was left empty - and an empty class name makes the engine try to load a class called "", which throws with an empty message and names neither the file nor the row. Every other item in that table fills the column; that is what proves it is required
- Fixed: the Grafton survivor's arithmetic. Eleven days against nineteen minutes is eight hundred and thirty-four times as long, not fifteen thousand - and being the person who worked that out is her entire characterisation

## v0.5.2 - "Battlegroup Discipline"
*"Devotion is not a substitute for stores. It never has been. It is simply discussed more often."*

- **The Covenant no longer starve themselves into rebellion.** Two markets demanding eleven food between them, producing none, and hostile to every faction in the sector so nothing could be imported. It was permanent by construction and it ended in unrest on both of their only worlds. This is the whole release
- **The Independents will trade with them.** Blanket hostility to everyone is what made the shortage unfixable, because it left no route by which anything could arrive. The Independents are not a power, have no stake in the war, and will sell to anyone holding hard currency - which a fleet that strips worlds has
- **An uncommissioned captain is no longer shot on sight.** World generation set the player hostile along with everybody else, so somebody carrying nobody's commission, who had never fired on them, could not dock, could not trade, and could never reach a line of the Ministry dialogue that has been in the mod since 0.5. Take a commission with their enemies and you are still an enemy, which is the part that was always meant
- **Sanctum had no spaceport.** None of any kind, worth roughly -110% accessibility by itself - the market was cut off from shipping entirely, including from the anchorage in its own system
- **Their mines finally feed something.** Sanctum produced ore, the anchorage's Orbital Works demanded metals, and no refinery existed anywhere in the faction, so the shipyard had been inert since it was added. There is a refinery now, and it stands at the mines rather than at the shipyard, because ore is heavy
- **Reclamation Vats.** A custom industry, because the Covenant do not farm and two of their holdings could not if they wished to. Stripped rock and reclaimed biomass in, protein paste and Unggoy methane out. Nutritionally complete, theologically sanctioned, and every species aboard agrees it is an insult
- **Sacrament Workshops.** The other half nobody made: domestic goods and drugs. What a stranded fleet manufactures instead of consumer goods is liturgy - harness and plate for five species that share no anatomy, censers, prayer strips, and the ration narcotics the Ministry issues by rank and declines to call narcotics
- **They can make their own fuel.** Fuel Production had demanded volatiles since it was added and nothing in the faction mined any. Sanctum now carries the seam, and the plant sits on top of it rather than a system away
- **Reverence has a relay net.** Epsilon Eridani has carried a comm relay, a nav buoy and a sensor array since 0.1 and the Covenant carried none of the three, which costs accessibility they could not spare
- **The battlegroup has moved five systems further out.** They sat close enough to Epsilon Eridani that a starting fleet drifting the obvious direction met a faction hostile to everything that fields nothing under a cruiser. They are not balanced as an early threat and were never meant to be
- **Sanctum is a size 7 forge world** rather than a size 5 mining camp, which is what its smelter needed to feed the fleet, and which upgrades its garrison to a High Command
- Their markets hold a stability floor while trade routes establish. A warfleet under military discipline does not riot about rations
- An economy audit writes the whole supply and demand picture to the log on every load. It exists because five separate diagnoses of this faction were made by reading industry lists and recalling what vanilla demands, and the measured numbers overturned something every single time - including which market the refinery belonged on, and the fact that the last bottleneck was workforce rather than ore

## v0.5.1
*"There's a file on you with a different letterhead to mine. I can't see all of it and I've stopped asking."*

- **The campaign has an intel screen now.** This is the whole point of the release. Ten of the twelve systems in 0.5 talked to you exclusively through messages that scroll past and are gone - your Section Zero file, your clearance, devotion, the relic count, the tech programmes, the directive you're running, and a clock counting down to a world being glassed. A world was about to burn and there was nowhere to go and look at that. Now there are four entries: an ONI personnel file, your Covenant standing, a glassing warning with a marker on the doomed world and a countdown that means something, and the Section Zero retirement detail
- **The liaison exists.** She's been speaking every ONI screen in this mod since 0.3 and has never been anybody - the desk text literally said she doesn't introduce herself. She's Commander Odile Ellis now, she sits at Reach, and past Ensign you get a frequency and can reach her from anywhere. She reads your file before she picks up, so the greeting changes depending on whether Section Zero has something open on you, whether there's a detail out with your name on the order, whether you called in with the transponder lit, and how far behind your directive you are
- **She'll tell you her name if you ask,** and only above Lieutenant Commander. It's one word, no rank and no first name, and that's the whole answer
- **The twelve Reach survivors can be answered.** They were the best writing in the mod and every one was a dead end - you walk in holding the registry for a hull somebody served in, they tell you something they haven't said in years, and the port menu comes back. Two follow-ups each, written per ship. Redoubtable's captain will tell you what the eleven minutes bought. Iroquois' won't tell you what was aft of the bridge. Coral Sea's asked you not to, and if you ask anyway you get a duty roster instead of a story
- **The flight recorders add up to something.** Six of them and the set completing was the only thing in the mod that promised something and then did nothing. Now each has a follow-up, her answers get less guarded as the set fills, and once all six are in the desk carries a standing question about what Section Three wanted them for that Fleet didn't. There's a deeper version of that answer behind omega clearance, because on the fifth recorder she says there's a personnel file she can't pull and to ask again if you ever hold it. It's the only place in the mod where a clearance buys prose instead of a purchase
- **The Council are five different species instead of one register.** All six have been carrying a line about where they were when the sky came apart over Reach since 0.5, and nothing has ever read it. The San'Shyuum won't say where the road goes. The Sangheili says the humans fight past the point where fighting is useful, and that he's said so in a chamber where it wasn't welcome. The Jiralhanae is patient and very good at counting. The Unggoy woke up and the world was orange and everyone was singing and he didn't know why
- **The Kig-Yar quartermaster uses contractions,** which nobody else on that side does. She's contracted to the Covenant, not of it
- **A codex.** Five entries under an Epsilon Eridani heading - how both fleets got here and what didn't come through with them, what the Cole Protocol actually requires and what it costs the person carrying it out, what a Luminary is, why the Covenant can't build a shipyard, and what glassing is. That last one is optimised for sterilising a surface rather than killing people, which is a distinction ONI took four years to write down because writing it down meant accepting the population was incidental
- **The Tours-class light cruiser,** from a three-view by **Azores-1994**, used with their permission. A gun cruiser - heavy spinal coilgun, four outrigger sponsons, no hangar. Sits between the Halcyon and the Marathon at 27 fleet points
- **Job boards say what a job needs before you take it.** Marines and running dark were both enforced at the moment you tried to carry the work out, and neither was stated when you accepted it. Flying nine jumps to be told you needed forty marines is the sort of thing people write forum posts about
- **Promotions say what the grade buys.** Twelve of them, and the screen where the most consequential number in the UNSC campaign changed had never mentioned what changed. There's a different answer if Section Zero has a file open - she's just signed for somebody another office is reading about, and she says so
- **Section One's assessments and the Vault's findings are readable.** Four each, unlocking off numbers that already existed. The Section One track was a number that went up and bought nothing; now it buys what the intelligence Section has actually worked out about the war, in the order a real analytical shop would work it out
- The suppression fleet stopped walking every star system and every fleet in the sector, once a day, to re-find a ship it already had a reference to
- Four new build checkers. One resolves loose-script imports into the mod's own jar, where a rename would silently disable a whole system. One reads sprite paths out of code as well as data. One catches two rule entries run together by a missing separator. One catches an option offered twice by two rules that both fire. The last two exist because each of them let something through this month
- A fifth checker verifies that rules.csv is actually what the generator produces - the generator broke once and every other checker stayed green, because they were all reading the last good file

**Fixes**
- **Fixed: the shadow tasking was repeatable and never required you to run dark.** A missing comma in the rule generator ran two conditions together into one nonsense condition that always passed, so the job lost its already-done guard and its covert requirement at the same time. It's been like that since the tasking was added
- **Fixed: two dialogue screens were the same button twice.** The comm menu offered its options through a trigger that fires every matching rule rather than the best one, so two rules that could both be true put the same four buttons on screen. Caught before it shipped, but only just
- Fixed: five screens where the liaison stopped using contractions. She does it in four lines out of five and had drifted on the clearance readouts, the Section One forecast and the last flight recorder
- Fixed: she turned the same slate at the top of four unrelated scenes

## v0.5 - "The Great Journey"
*"You have been raised up. Do not mistake that for being trusted."*

- **The Covenant have a campaign now.** That was the whole point of this release. Last version they had 34 hulls and a grudge; now their commission puts you in a seven-grade Hierarchy - Minor through to Imperial Admiral - earned in devotion for fighting their war and quietly eroding when you stop. Rank is asked for at the Ministry and it decides what they'll let you touch
- A Council of six. Three San'Shyuum, three Sangheili, and a Jiralhanae nobody can quite account for. They're the face of every Covenant screen in the mod, and one of them turns out to matter a great deal later
- The Luminary Array does something now. It was already a ship system on their hulls. Carry one and Forerunner relics start turning up in ordinary salvage - every wreck and ruin already scattered around the sector, no hand-placed sites, so it works in a random sector as well as in Corvus. You can't sell them to anybody. They buy standing, and something is counting
- The tithe. Hand your holds over at the anchorage for credits and devotion. It's the only economy they offer you, because they're not about to let you run a trade fleet
- **ONI is three Sections instead of one shop.** One deals in intelligence, Two in influence, Three in clearance, and you earn them by doing different things. Red, black and omega clearance now answer to deniable jobs you've actually finished rather than to your rank number, so buying grades stopped quietly buying the secrets. Old saves get backfilled from whatever their rank used to imply
- Sections One and Two finally sell something. Both were earned and neither bought anything, which made them two numbers that went up. They're desks now, with catalogues, and they compete: a request granted is a budget spent and it came out of one of the other two. Buy enough from one and the others start charging you for it
- **Section Zero.** The Section that audits the other three. It keeps a file on you built from what you actually did - deniable work taken, UNSC tonnage you personally destroyed, Covenant hardware you kept instead of handing in, hulls in your fleet no yard has a record of building. Five bands, and it escalates: an audit you can talk your way through, then clearance frozen, then somebody asking your contacts careful questions, and at the end a detail sent to retire you. They fly UNSC hulls, because they are UNSC, and your commission won't help
- Standing orders. Section Three asks you for things now instead of only selling to you. Sized against a live reading of how much of the sector the Covenant hold - 40 fleet points of shipping when they're contained, 160 when they're not - and it counts your engagements whether or not you remember it's running
- **A fleet operations board,** which is where most of the "what do I do next" problem got solved. Fourteen kinds of line work from derelict sweeps up to theatre command, gated by service grade rather than clearance. The deniable board works abroad; this is the war at home. It's also the only thing that pays Section One, which previously had no source at all
- The top of that ladder is new. Task force command hands you three hulls on loan - and losing them goes in a file you don't control. Above that, a standing detachment maintained out of your stipend, and theatre orders, where you don't fly anything and other people's fleets move because you signed something
- **Captured technology.** Covenant salvage comes off tonnage you actually put out of the fight, and three programmes turn it into a containment lattice, a Luminary sensor suite, and a tap into an energy projector feed that nobody signed off on. Those hullmods have no other source. No shop, no cache, no blueprint
- Prize crews. Win a fight with marines aboard and you can put a crew on something you disabled and keep it. 40 marines for a frigate, 320 for a capital, and it arrives at ten percent readiness, which is about what a boarding action leaves you
- The quiet jobs are actually quiet now. Shadowing, dead drops, infiltration, extraction and sabotage used to resolve exactly like a courier run - fly in with the transponder lit and a patrol on your wing, click, collect. They won't go ahead if you're being watched. Kill the transponder, lose whoever's on you, come back
- **The front line moves.** Three worlds came through the gate already burned and that used to be the end of it. Now the Covenant build a suppression force, sail it across the sector, hold station over a world and burn it. Intercept it in transit, break it in orbit, or lose the planet. My first attempt at this was a countdown, which was a bad idea - a clock you can't see or shoot at is an announcement, not a threat
- They can take ground, too. colonyExpeditionChance has been zero since they were added, on the grounds that a fleet with no shipyard doesn't go colonising. That stopped being true when Sanctum became a forge world. It's 0.25 now, well under the UNSC's 0.8 - humanity colonises, the Covenant occupy what they've already burned
- **The Great Schism.** Get senior enough to be told and carry enough relics to have earned an answer, and Rtas 'Kaidonee will explain what the Luminary has actually been reading, and who the honour guard got handed to last month. Stand with the Hierarchy or walk. Walking is the first way out of the Covenant this mod has ever had
- **Six new missions,** two of them Covenant-side, which is also a first. Glassing puts you in the CCS over a colony that can't win. Cole's Gambit can't be won either and isn't meant to be. Boarding Action, Picket Line, Prize of War, and Schism, which is twenty minutes of Sangheili against Covenant with a UNSC squadron holding station at the edge of the plot doing nothing at all
- **Every UNSC ship lost its shields.** Not reduced. Gone. The salvaged Covenant emitters are out and the hullmod that described them is retired, because it had ended up attached to precisely nothing. What's left is Titanium-A and the armour scheme it's bolted to
- Archer salvos went from thirty-two missiles to eight. The Infinity could erase the biggest thing the Covenant own in two volleys. I picked the replacement number by measuring volleys-to-kill against every equal-strength opponent in the fleet rather than guessing, which I should have done the first time
- The Covenant stopped being outgunned by arithmetic. Their hulls were never the problem. The UNSC take 80-85% of their firepower from built-in weapons that cost no ordnance points; the Covenant took ten percent, so they were paying for guns the UNSC got free. Their canon torpedo banks are built in now and their mount counts match UNSC hulls the same size
- And they field the right ships. Their hull frequencies were upside down - 3.0 on every frigate against 0.7 on nine of their ten capitals, which meant the human navy was more likely to put a capital on the board than the aliens were. 34% of their draws were cruiser-or-bigger. It's 73% now
- The battlegroup can garrison its own system. Of their two markets only the anchorage had any industry that puts fleets in space. Reverence has a High Command now like Reach does, Sanctum has a garrison, and both are a size larger. Old saves get brought up to the same standard on load
- Every icon in the mod is the mod's own. Nine of eleven cargo items and six of seven hullmods were wearing vanilla art, one icon was doing double duty for two different things, and every campaign system stamped its intel with the same faction crest, so a boarding action and a glassing looked identical in the log. Thirty-seven new pieces
- Weapons with no mount art can't be looted, bought or sold any more. Most of them are spinal guns drawn into the ship sprite, so a turret sprite would be a second gun on top of the first - fine on the hull, silly in a cargo hold
- Ship prices rebuilt from combat power on both sides
- The UNSC have a shipRoles block, which they never did. Their fleets were being assembled from raw hull frequency, so a fleet tanker was as likely as a missile destroyer to end up escorting a supercarrier
- Weapon groups rebuilt on eighty-nine variants using the game's own auto-assign. Point defence had been scattered across gun groups on sixty-eight of them, and built-in weapons sat in no group at all, so nothing gave you a key to fire a spinal projector with

**Fixes**
- **Fixed: none of the Covenant weapons fired.** This one was entirely my fault and it took a bug report to find. Baking the engine's auto-assign into the goal variants put 128 non-PD Covenant weapons on autofire, where every hand-written variant had them on manual. They carry nothing but energy weapons with high per-shot flux, so left alone they emptied the bank into the first thing in arc, hard-locked, and stopped
- Fixed: Covenant turrets couldn't turn. Six of their weapons had no turn rate at all, which the loader reads as zero. Welded in place, pointing wherever the slot left them. The fuel rod alone was on 54 turret mounts
- Fixed: fuel rods fired and vanished. Same weapon, no flight time either. The torpedo expired on the frame it spawned
- **Fixed: half the ONI desk was unreachable.** The game numbers dialogue options one to nine and then stops. The desk had fifteen. It's grouped now, seven at the top and nothing more than two steps away. Thirty-four screens were also rebuilding the port's own menu over their options, which had been true of the Ministry since the day it went in
- Fixed: standing orders never arrived. The mechanism was fine, the schedule wasn't. The first directive could only issue when a twenty-four to thirty-four *day* interval elapsed, and if you weren't commissioned at the moment it landed the whole month was spent and the wait started again. It failed silently too, which is why it took someone playing it to notice
- Fixed: a repeatable payout. You could claim a completed job as many times as you liked, because the check tested what kind of job it was and not whether that particular one was already done
- Fixed: two stations showing up in the custom fleet picker
- Fixed: 200 Covenant mount pairs carrying one weapon to port and a different one to starboard, and 35 mounts across the fleet with no opposite number at all
- Fixed: two campaign scripts rebuilding the sector's entire market list five times a tick

- **Fixed: v0.4 would not launch at all.** Two separate load-time faults, both in Covenant content added late in the release, and both entirely mine
- The Plasma Lance declared a pierce set of ASTEROIDS, MISSILES and FIGHTERS. None of those are values the engine recognises - the real ones are PROJECTILE_FF, MISSILE_NO_FF and so on - and an unrecognised one is not ignored, it throws. The game died in the weapon loader every time, before the main menu, with an error naming only the bad word
- Three Covenant ship systems then pointed their stats scripts at com.fs.starfarer.api.impl.combat. Two of the mod's systems legitimately do live there, which is what made the other three look right - but Fortress Shield, High Energy Focus and Burn Drive are loose scripts under the core data folder instead. Same crash, one loader later
- Because the game loads weapons and systems alphabetically, each fault hid the next. So rather than fix the file that crashed, I went back through all 36 weapons and all 12 ship systems against what vanilla actually uses. That is where the second and third came from
- **The real lesson is that none of this was caught by the mod's own checks.** The suite has eleven of them and every one passed on a build that could not start, because they all verify that ids point at things that exist and none of them verified enum values, script paths outside the mod's own namespace, or that a CSV's quotes balance. All three are checked now, and each new check was tested by deliberately reintroducing the bug to confirm it fails
- **Groundwork: ONI has three Sections rather than one ladder.** Section One deals in intelligence, Section Two in influence, Section Three in clearance, and they are earned separately. Black and omega clearance now answer to deniable work actually done rather than to the rank number, so buying grades no longer quietly buys the secrets - red still comes with standing, because it is the way in. Existing saves keep the clearance their rank implied. Not much of this is visible yet; it is the spine the next release hangs off

## v0.4 - "First Contact"
*"They are not lost. They know exactly where they are. They are here, and they intend to be here next as well."*

- **The Covenant are playable.** 34 hulls - 24 warships from the DAV light corvette up to the CAS assault carrier, a tithe freighter, five strike craft, and four fixed installations. They open hostile to every faction in the sector, the UNSC and the player included, and there is no diplomatic way out of it. That part is on purpose
- **Twelve Covenant weapons.** Energy projectors and plasma lances on the capitals, plasma torpedoes that track, pulse lasers in three weights, needlers, fuel rod projectors, and Shade emplacements for close work. All energy, none of it shared with the UNSC, and none of it buyable from anyone else
- **Five Covenant ship systems.** Screen Surge, Barrier Focus, Lance Overcharge, Slipspace Surge and the Luminary Array. When I first got the fleet into the game it had one system and a borrowed hullmod between all thirty-four hulls, which made a whole faction play like about four ships
- **Energy Projection Screens.** The Covenant original of the shield the UNSC salvages. Cheaper to hold, faster to re-form, and with a lot less hull behind it - they will outlast a sustained gunline and then fold the moment it gets through. The UNSC hullmod is the same equipment cut out of a wreck and grafted onto armour that was never designed to carry it
- **The Covenant are armed out of the books as well.** Same method the UNSC got in v0.3: mount counts scaled from canonical armament instead of picked to look sensible. The SDV and CCS have full published tables, the CAS is half-documented, and the remaining twenty-two are mod originals scaled against whichever class they most resemble. Which is which is written down in the mod, so nothing I invented gets passed off as canon
- **Reverence.** The battlegroup's anchorage - forge world, asteroid belt, and two tiers of station assembled the way the game actually builds stations, a hub carrying lance, cradle and dock modules. My first attempt was one enormous single hull and it crashed the game the moment anything approached it
- **Custom portraits, at last.** Nine UNSC faces and nine Covenant ones, replacing the vanilla portraits the mod has been borrowing since 0.1. Both sets are selectable in character creation
- **Your portrait chooses your name.** Pick a UNSC face and the game generates a UNSC name for you; pick a Covenant face and it generates a Covenant one. Every officer and fleet commander either faction produces is named the same way, so a Covenant patrol is not commanded by someone called Gary any more
- Two new missions, bringing it to five. Battlegroup sets a UNSC line squadron against a CCS and its escorts; First Contact is the engagement that started all of this
- Housekeeping: 124 orphaned files removed - duplicate sprites, art for weapons retired two versions ago, sounds nothing referenced. Five reference copies of vanilla data the game never loaded are out of the mod folder, and a hullmod retired in v0.3 that had survived as a commented-out row is now gone properly

**Fixes**
- **Fixed: Sabre and Longsword wings would close to attack and then not shoot.** Their guns were declared on the hull as built-in weapons. Not one of vanilla's thirty-two fighters does that - the guns belong to the wing's variant, and until they were moved there the fighters flew into range and sat looking at the target. Reported on the forum, and correct
- **Fixed: the Hillsborough had no MAC at all, and the Infinity was missing four of hers along with ten Onagers.** Also reported on the forum, also correct, and particularly embarrassing on a mod whose whole pitch is canonical armament
- **Fixed: accepting a UNSC commission could hard-crash the game.** The commission bonus pointed at a hullmod retired back in v0.3. Starsector only treats a CSV row as commented out when the line itself begins with a #, and every validation tool I had was quietly reading those rows as live data - so the reference looked perfectly healthy right up to the moment a player clicked accept
- **Fixed: the Covenant showed up in Nexerelin with no ships to choose from.** The engine builds its hull-to-variant map at load and drops any hull whose list comes back empty, which is every hull whose variants ship inside the mod. Repaired at load now
- **Fixed: opening a Covenant market, or meeting a Covenant fleet, threw a null and ended the run.** One missing letter in a music id - the faction asked for music_pirate_* where the game has music_pirates_*. A missing track only matters at the instant something tries to play it, which is why one typo surfaced as two apparently unrelated crashes in two completely different screens
- **Fixed: Covenant portraits appeared twice each in character creation.** All nine were listed under both genders, and the creator merges the two lists

## v0.3.1
- **UNSC ships fight bow-first now.** The Barrier Emitters have always made a hit on the prow cost about half what one from astern does - the power runs are laid through the bow armour belt, which is why it is thick forward and thin aft. Nothing in the combat AI knew that, so UNSC ships fought broadside like everyone else and quietly threw away their best defence. They now keep the armoured prow toward whatever is shooting
- **The MAC has to earn its shot.** A spinal gun running most of the length of the hull will not fire until it has held a target in its arc long enough to have a solution - about two seconds, and it decays faster than it builds if they slide out of the arc. Once locked it stays locked while they stay in front of you. Swinging the bow across a target and snapping off a MAC round does not work any more, which is rather the point
- **Reach and Tribute are glassed.** The Covenant took both in 2552, and the gate brought the scars along with the planets. Long stretches of surface are fused to glass and the survivors live in the gaps between them. It costs hazard and stability - these worlds are held, not recovered

**Fixes**
- **Fixed: a fighter wing of more than six craft crashed the combat UI.** The engine indexes fighters into a fixed array and vanilla never exceeds six, which turned out to be a limit rather than a convention. Every wing is capped there now, and the checker suite rejects anything larger - it failed inside the HUD rather than at load, so nothing in the crash pointed anywhere near the wing data
- **Fixed: skin variants were shipping unarmed.** A skin's loadout names the skin's own hull id, which has no hull file behind it, so the tool that fills empty mounts silently skipped every one of them. The Able went to war with nothing fitted at all
- Fixed: a mirrored pair of mounts could carry two different weapons, which looks wrong and fights worse. Five pairs across the Artemis, Geneva, Point Blank and Strident, plus the Gladius, which was two ordnance points short of arming its own mirrors

## v0.3 - "Displacement"
*"A frigate that survives what a frigate shouldn't survive isn't a good frigate. It's a destroyer with the wrong paperwork."*

- **The fleet is armed out of the books now.** Mount counts used to come from modding guidelines. That kept everything tidy next to vanilla and made the fleet look nothing like Halo. A canon Stalwart carries 92 missile pods and 58 point-defence guns; ours had four mounts and not a single gun on it. Every warship has been rebuilt from Halopedia's own armament tables
- The upshot is that ships finally feel like themselves. The Marathon is a missile boat. The Strident is a gunship. The Punic is a wall of point defence. The Poseidon-pattern carriers barely shoot at all and aren't supposed to
- Twenty-three ships had a proper armament listed to work from. Two are only half-documented, and seven are mod originals with no canon at all, so those borrow from whatever class they most resemble - the Thanatos fights like a Marathon, the Eion like a Vindication. It's all written down, so nothing I made up gets passed off as canon
- The counts are scaled rather than literal. Canon runs from fourteen weapons on a Charon to two thousand two hundred on an Infinity, and nothing sensible survives that kind of spread. The Infinity is still far and away the most heavily armed thing in the sector, at 96 mounts against its old 26
- The new missile pods are built in, which is the trick that makes any of this work. Built-ins don't cost ordnance points, so a ship can carry twenty pods without eating into the loadout you actually choose. The guns are still yours. Spinal MACs weren't touched
- **Bigger fighter and bomber wings.** Every wing that was under the engine's ceiling has been raised to it, so a Baselard, Broadsword or Sabre wing is now six craft. An Infinity puts twenty-two aircraft up, an Epoch eighteen. UNSC carriers finally launch something that looks like an air group
- **Frigates and destroyers were quietly overpowered.** The canon rescale made them bigger without making them any more expensive to field, so you were getting destroyer toughness at frigate prices. Twelve hulls now cost a little more and take a little less. Cruisers and capitals were fine as they were
- **The fleet had its flux dissipation backwards.** Frigates and destroyers vented faster than vanilla while cruisers and capitals choked - exactly the wrong way round for a navy whose capitals are meant to be the frightening part. The big ships are brought up to par; the small ones were already generous
- **The Strident had the best shields in the fleet, by accident.** Every other UNSC destroyer sits between 1.00 and 1.20 shield efficiency. The Strident was on 0.60, which is Paragon grade, on a hull that already carries heavy armour. It soaked more than twice what any of its siblings could for the same price. It now matches the Charon, Paris and Hillsborough
- **The Halcyon (D) went to war with one gun.** The scrapyard refit had a single point-defence cluster fitted across eleven mounts. It's meant to be a worn-out ship - the knackered engines and dodgy flux grid say that already. It wasn't meant to be unarmed
- **The ONI prowlers were missing from every UNSC fleet.** Razor, Winter, Point Blank and Sahara are phase ships, and the faction was set never to field phase ships. They'd been sitting in the fleet tables the whole time, never once spawning
- Those four were also carrying the shield hullmod, on ships that have no shields. It did precisely nothing. Gone
- **New at the Section Three desk: "What am I cleared for?"** Red, black and omega clearance gate the job board, the cells, all six deniable contracts and both fleet abilities - and the game never once mentioned they existed. Now the desk tells you which you hold, what each opens, and what you need for the next one
- **The survivors recognise their old ships.** Pull a wreck out of Epsilon Eridani, get a crew aboard, then dock where its survivor is posted. They'll notice. Twelve scenes, one per officer, no two the same. Ines Marchetti wants to know whether the Leviathan still pulls three degrees to port. Rurik Halloran has some thoughts about the word "redoubtable" on a ship that lasted eleven minutes
- **The "Spartan" laser line is gone.** Directed energy is Covenant technology. The UNSC shoots mass drivers, autocannons and missiles. All three were fitted to nothing anyway
- The ARES torpedo and the M96 rocket pod go with them. The missile lineup is Scythe, Archer, Archer Heavy and Shiva, all built in, and there isn't a UNSC hull with a slot you could put a bought launcher into. UNSC AI Integration is retired too - Shipboard AI replaced it long ago
- **Eight ships had a collision outline that crossed over itself.** It looks like nothing in the file and makes collisions behave oddly. No vanilla ship does it. Eight of ours did
- **Thirteen weapon mounts weren't actually on the ship.** Some hung over empty space, some sat off the side of the hull entirely. Marathon, Artemis, Baselard, Thermopylae and four station modules. Moved in pairs, so nothing came out lopsided
- Rewrote the Marathon, Punic and Sabre codex entries. All three were about half the length of everything else, and the Marathon especially reads like a gun cruiser when it's a fourteen-cell missile boat

**Fixes**
- **Fixed: a UNSC world could end up with no governor at all, permanently.** The game marked a world as sorted before it had actually put anyone in the chair, and that mark is the thing that stops it trying again. Fixed, and any world already stuck gets put right when you load

## v0.2.1 - "No Such Office"
*"If he transmits before you close, this office was never here and neither were you."*

- **Requisition chits.** ONI pays in them, and they sit in your hold like anything else, so what you're owed is never hidden behind a counter you can't see
- **The full UNSC officer ladder**, Warrant Officer up to Fleet Admiral. Twelve grades, bought with chits, each one raising a monthly stipend so long as you hold a UNSC commission. The first three you earn rather than buy - they arrive at 30, 40 and 50 reputation, no chits and no commission needed
- A requisition desk that opens up as you climb: ODST cadres, battleplate patterns, Smart AI cores, black archives, the capital programme. Get past Rear Admiral and it stops selling blueprints and starts handing over finished hulls - a Halcyon, an Autumn, a Punic-II, out of fleet reserve with nothing on paper saying where they went
- Standing and service are separate things. You can have either, both or neither. The stipend needs a commission, which you pick up the ordinary way through the comm directory
- **An ONI job board** - four jobs up at a time, always kept full. Courier runs, dead drops, prisoner transfers, infiltration, extraction and sabotage, settled on the ground with marines rather than guns. Take as many as you like; they each sit on a different world
- The board replaced the operatives' old contact missions, which handed out one to three jobs a month each and capped you at five contacts across the whole sector. Ordinary contacts work normally on UNSC worlds again as a result
- **Six deniable contracts** behind those three clearances: a defecting ONI commander, a prowler that mustn't be recovered, a warlord flying stolen UNSC hulls, a Tri-Tachyon charter reading our Smart AI research, a black site gone quiet, and a Vice Admiral who never disobeyed an order. Nearly every target flies UNSC hardware, so black ops means fighting your own ships
- **Undercover cells** in the capital of every faction in the sector. They're found by reading the conditions the game itself uses to mark a seat of power, so it works with modded factions and randomised sectors. You won't find one on a menu - at Lieutenant Commander somebody starts waiting in the bar, and the door only opens after that drink
- Cells deal in locally "adjusted" fleet stores, quiet edits to your record in the host's own files, funding that turns credits into standing, and whatever nine months of deep cover has managed to put aside. A capital that gets invaded or decivilised moves its cell on its own
- **Every UNSC world and station is run by someone who got off one of those wrecks.** Twelve named officers, each tied to a specific hull in the graveyard, the senior ones on the bigger worlds
- **The Epsilon Eridani graveyard is worth visiting for more than parts.** Every named hull still has its flight recorder aboard. Hand them in and, across the six of them, Section Three tells you a piece at a time what it's willing to admit about how those ships died
- **EMCON** - shuts down every emitter the fleet doesn't need to keep moving. Your profile drops to 35% and your own sensor range with it, but you keep your speed. Going dark buys quiet with speed; EMCON buys it with your eyes
- **The Cole Protocol** - purge the navigation databases and the fleet drops to a 15% profile for a day and a half, far below anything else going, at the cost of 10% combat readiness across every ship. It's what you do when being followed home is worse than arriving in a state
- Neither is free at the start. EMCON comes with red clearance at Ensign, the Cole Protocol with black at Lieutenant Commander, so the ladder buys you capability and not just money
- **Every hull's armament rebuilt from its canonical loadout.** Halopedia's tables for thirteen classes come out at roughly half missile pods, half point defence and almost no conventional guns. The mod was nearly all gun mounts, which is the wrong navy entirely
- No two classes fight alike any more. Slot positions didn't change, so total firepower is the same - only its character moved
- Archer and Scythe pods are built into the hulls rather than fitted. UNSC launchers don't draw a turret, so a mount holding one looked empty in the refit screen. They cost no ordnance now and can't be stripped off

**Fixes**
- **Fixed: ONI missions never paid out.** The script was looking for the wrong kind of mission entirely, so it never found any of them and no chits were ever handed over
- **Fixed: only the first ONI mission ever paid.** Finished jobs were being marked off by the type of job rather than the job itself, so each kind paid exactly once and everything after that came up empty
- **Fixed: the standing screen lied when it had nothing to say**, cheerfully telling a player whose standing hadn't loaded yet that they were already Fleet Admiral
- **Fixed: UNSC fleet commanders had no skills at all.** The faction was handing them officer skills where only admiral skills are accepted, so every single one was thrown away
- **Fixed: UNSC ports only sold other people's ships.** The game's dockside offer never checks whose port it is, so a UNSC world would happily try to sell you a Buffalo. Their yards now put their own decommissioned hulls up
- Fixed a mangled Paris description that was rendering as black diamonds in the codex

## v0.1 - "Fall of Reach"
First release of UNSC Reborn. The numbering starts fresh rather than carrying on from the original's
0.4a; everything below is what changed against that mod.

- Every hull sized from its canonical length, with the proportions between classes kept intact
- Sprites brightened across the board - they'd been rendering close to black
- Mounts spread along the hulls instead of bunched at the bow, and 60 added to the classes that were visibly under-armed
- The Sahara heavy prowler was flying backwards. It isn't any more
- The Spirit of Fire is a unique named ship rather than something you can build
- **Salvaged Barrier Emitters** - shields that wrap the whole hull, thick across the bow and thin astern
- M58 Archer pods fire 32-missile salvos, with exhaust and contrails
- Weapon ranges and the flux economy rebalanced against the new hull sizes; Shipboard AI built into every hull at no cost; Sabre, Baselard and Broadsword wings doubled
- **Epsilon Eridani**, with recoverable wrecks, named hulls carrying built-in refits, salvage and a debris field
- ONI Section Three operatives as the faction's only contacts, four custom industries, four colony items, and Anchor stations in three tiers
- The faction is fully self-contained - no vanilla blueprints, weapons or ships anywhere in it
- Removed a bundled copy of Console Commands, and a debug script that handed the player a free fleet on load
- Station modules had weapons bigger than their mounts and were rendering detached. Fixed
